# Worlds and perspectives

A fictional world offers a concrete way to explore bounded intelligence. Code tracks locations, inventory, time, and established events. Models supply character judgments, dialogue, and descriptions. An application decides which proposals become canonical changes.

## Knowledge has a location

A character receives information it witnessed, learned, or can legitimately retrieve. Attributed rumors should remain distinct from established facts. A gossip can spread an accusation without acquiring authority to establish the accused person's guilt.

Fresh model contexts and explicit inputs provide a useful foundation. A perspective reagent selects appropriate records for a character. A world-state reagent applies authorized transitions and reject impossible changes.

Two properties require different evaluation:

**Information isolation** concerns which records the runtime supplied and which operations it allowed. **Believable ignorance** concerns whether a model behaves consistently with that limited perspective. A model may guess hidden facts, rely on training knowledge, or invent unsupported details even when the context boundary works correctly.

Perfect context selection therefore does not prove perfect character behavior. Canonical changes need their own checks and acceptance policy.

## Watch the machinery

A player view shows dialogue and consequences. A backstage view reveals the rituals, supplied information, proposals, accepted changes, and resource use. Delay spoiler-bearing evidence until after play or make it an explicit spectator mode.

A small setting is enough: several residents, competing goals, a secret, and a few days of events. A human game master accepts world changes while using Summonr for background activity. The experience demonstrates context, authority, continuity, and evidence through something people can directly understand.

The resulting traces also support research into continuity and knowledge leakage, with explicit player consent for research contributions. The game remains an application of the machinery, not a replacement for evaluating its guarantees.
